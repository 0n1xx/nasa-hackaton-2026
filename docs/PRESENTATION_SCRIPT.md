# Before the Smoke: five-minute pitch script

Three speakers. About 560 spoken words. Ends at about 4:40, leaving roughly 20 seconds of margin for the live demo.

- Words in plain text are spoken.
- Stage directions are in [BRACKETS]. Never read them aloud.
- Labels in **bold** are the exact words on screen. Point at them as you say them.
- Timestamps are where you should be. If you are ahead, slow down. If you are behind, cut the line marked *(cut if late)*.

| Speaker | Job | On stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speaker 1 | The hook and the problem. Comes back to close. | 0:00–1:15, then 4:22–4:40 |
| Speaker 2 | The solution. Comes back for the ask. | 1:15–2:20, then 3:50–4:22 |
| Speaker 3 | The live demo. | 2:20–3:50 |

## SPEAKER 1: the hook and the problem (0:00–1:15)

**[SLIDE 1: black screen, white text: "What percentage of Canada's population is Indigenous?"]**

**[Walk to the centre. Wait until the room is quiet. Then speak slowly.]**

**0:00** Do you know what percentage of Canada's population is Indigenous?

**[PAUSE. Ten full seconds. Count them silently. Look at faces. Do not fill the silence. If someone calls out a number, nod and keep waiting.]**

**0:14** Five percent.

**[PAUSE 2 seconds. Speaker 2 advances to SLIDE 2: "5%" alone.]**

**0:18** And that five percent accounts for about forty-two percent of the wildfire evacuation events in this country.

**[Speaker 2 brings in "42%" beside "5%", larger. PAUSE 3 seconds.]**

**0:29** In July, it happened again. Fires north of Thunder Bay forced five First Nations out of their homes. Whitesand. Gull Bay. Collins. Families fled to Thunder Bay, and some slept in their cars because there were no hotel rooms left.

**0:44** Why them? It's not a coincidence. Many reserves were put on land settlers didn't want. Remote, forested, one road in, or none. Today, people living on reserve are about eight times more likely to live in a high wildfire-risk zone.

**[PAUSE 1 second.]**

**1:03** And this is ancestral land. People were moved once already. They shouldn't have to keep leaving. We can't stop every wildfire. But what if we could see one coming?

**[Turn and look at Speaker 2. Step back. No "over to you".]**

---

## SPEAKER 2: the solution (1:15–2:20)

**[Speaker 1 advances to SLIDE 3: "Before the Smoke" and the line "See it coming. Ask for help before anyone has to leave."]**

**1:15** We can. Fires give warning signs. They just come in pieces.

**1:20** The heat climbs. The air dries out. The rain stops. The wind picks up. Satellites spot new heat on the ground.

**[PAUSE 1 second.]**

**1:30** Before the Smoke puts those pieces together: NASA satellite fire detections, NASA photos, weather forecasts, and forty-two years of NASA weather records.

**1:41** For every First Nation in Canada, it tells you, in plain words, how high the fire risk is for the coming days, and why.

**1:49** Then it helps the community act. What to do now. And a request for firefighters and equipment, written for them, addressed to the people who can send them.

**2:02** The goal is not a faster evacuation. It's fewer of them.

**[PAUSE 1 second.]**

**2:08** To be clear, it predicts the risk, not the exact spot a fire starts. Let us show you.

**[Turn to Speaker 3, who is already at the laptop. Speaker 1 switches the screen to the browser.]**

---

## SPEAKER 3: the live demo (2:20–3:50)

**[BEFORE GOING ON STAGE: browser full screen (F11), zoom 100%, site open at the home page (**Find your community**), header toggle on **Example scenario**, search box empty.]**

### Step 1: find your community (2:20–2:32)

**2:20** This is Before the Smoke. It's November, so we're showing an example: a summer day, July fourteenth, the week of the real evacuations. The banner says so on every screen.

**[CLICK the search box, TYPE "White", CLICK **Whitesand First Nation**.]**

**2:27** It starts the way a community member would: find your community.

> *If search is slow:* click **Whitesand First Nation** in the **Highest fire risk** list instead.

### Step 2: plain words first (2:32–2:58)

**[Point at the first sentence on the left.]**

**2:32** Whitesand First Nation. Fire risk is very high today, and expected to stay very high for the next three days.

**2:40** How sure are we? It says so honestly: not very sure, and why.

**2:45** And why the risk is high: ten degrees hotter than last week, dry air, no soaking rain for six days. And from forty-two years of NASA records, today is hotter than ninety-six of every hundred mid-July days since 1984.

> *If the page shows "Couldn't load":* click **Example scenario** in the top right. Still failing: play the screen recording.

### Step 3: ask it anything (2:58–3:12)

**[CLICK **Who can send firefighters?** in the right-hand column.]**

**3:00** Anyone can ask a question. It answers from the same data, instantly, even offline: call 911 first, report the fire, and who to contact for firefighters and equipment.

### Step 4: the real NASA photo (3:12–3:28)

**[CLICK **Satellite photo** at the top of the map. Wait about two seconds.]**

**3:14** And this part is real. NASA's satellite photo from that same day, July fourteenth, when Whitesand and four other Nations were being evacuated. The red is fire seen from space. The haze is smoke.

> *If the photo doesn't draw within five seconds:* say the line anyway, click **Map**, and move on.

### Step 5: ask for help (3:28–3:50)

**[CLICK the blue **Ask for help** button at the bottom left.]**

**3:30** And this is the point. One click writes the request: firefighters ready nearby, pumps and sprinklers, addressed to the province and Indigenous Services Canada, with the evidence attached. Nothing is sent for them. The community decides, and sends it. Days before a fire, not after.

**[Let the page sit for two seconds. Switch to SLIDE 4 and step away from the laptop.]**

### If the laptop or projector fails completely (about 25 seconds instead of the demo)

Speaker 3 stays calm and says this instead, then hands straight to Speaker 2:

> Our laptop has other plans, so picture it. You type your community: Whitesand First Nation. In plain words it says fire risk is very high today and for the next three days, how sure we are, and why: ten degrees hotter in a week, dry air, no soaking rain. Then one button writes the request for firefighters and pumps, addressed to the province and Indigenous Services Canada. The community sends it. Days before a fire, not after.

You will finish about a minute early. Use the time to slow down. Do not add new material.

## SPEAKER 2: the ask (3:50–4:22)

**[SLIDE 4 is up: "5% of the population. 42% of wildfire evacuation events." Speaker 2 steps forward.]**

**3:50** So here's what we're asking for.

**3:53** One. Fund the before. Canada's Auditor General found that 78 percent of emergency spending for First Nations goes to response and recovery, after the fact. Put resources where the risk is lining up, before the fire.

**4:04** Two. Build tools like this with Indigenous leadership, not for them. Communities should own their data.

**4:11** Three. Treat this data as a shared responsibility. Open, honest about what it can't do, and in the hands of the people who need it.

**[Step back. Look at Speaker 1.]**

---

## SPEAKER 1: the close (4:22–4:40)

**4:22** Remember the number from the start? Five percent of the population. Forty-two percent of the evacuations.

**[PAUSE 2 seconds.]**

**4:32** The fires will come. Whether people see them coming, and get to stay home, is up to us.

**[Hold eye contact. Do not say thank you. Wait for the applause. END about 4:40.]**
