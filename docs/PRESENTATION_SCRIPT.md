# Before the Smoke: five-minute pitch script

Three speakers. About 540 spoken words. Ends at about 4:40, leaving roughly 20 seconds of margin for the live demo.

- Words in plain text are spoken.
- Stage directions are in [BRACKETS]. Never read them aloud.
- Labels in **bold** are the exact words on screen. Point at them as you say them.
- Timestamps are where you should be. If you are ahead, slow down. If you are behind, cut the line marked *(cut if late)*.

| Speaker | Job | On stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speaker 1 | The hook and the problem. Comes back to close. | 0:00–1:15, then 4:22–4:40 |
| Speaker 2 | The solution. Comes back for the ask. | 1:15–2:20, then 3:50–4:22 |
| Speaker 3 | The live demo. | 2:20–3:50 |

## SPEAKER 1: the hook and the problem (0:00–1:15)

**[SLIDE 1: black screen, white text: "What percentage of Canada's population is Indigenous?"]**

**[Walk to the centre. Wait until the room is quiet. Then speak slowly.]**

**0:00** Do you know what percentage of Canada's population is Indigenous?

**[PAUSE. Ten full seconds. Count them silently. Look at faces. Do not fill the silence. If someone calls out a number, nod and keep waiting.]**

**0:14** Five percent.

**[PAUSE 2 seconds. Speaker 2 advances to SLIDE 2: "5%" alone.]**

**0:18** And that five percent accounts for about forty-two percent of the wildfire evacuation events in this country.

**[Speaker 2 brings in "42%" beside "5%", larger. PAUSE 3 seconds.]**

**0:29** In July, it happened again. Fires north of Thunder Bay forced five First Nations out of their homes. Whitesand. Gull Bay. Collins. Families fled to Thunder Bay, and some slept in their cars because there were no hotel rooms left.

**0:44** Why them? It's not a coincidence. Many reserves were put on land settlers didn't want. Remote, forested, one road in, or none. Today, people living on reserve are about eight times more likely to live in a high wildfire-risk zone.

**[PAUSE 1 second.]**

**1:03** And this is ancestral land. People were moved once already. They shouldn't have to keep leaving. We can't stop every wildfire. But what if we could see one coming?

**[Turn and look at Speaker 2. Step back. No "over to you".]**

---

## SPEAKER 2: the solution (1:15–2:20)

**[Speaker 1 advances to SLIDE 3: "Before the Smoke" and the line "See fire risk building. Act before anyone has to leave."]**

**1:15** We can. Fires give warning signs. They just come in pieces.

**1:20** The heat climbs. The air dries out. The rain stops. The wind picks up. Satellites spot new heat on the ground.

**[PAUSE 1 second.]**

**1:30** Before the Smoke puts those pieces in one place: NASA satellite fire detections and imagery, weather, air quality, and years of fire history.

**1:41** When the signs line up, it flags that area as high risk. Days ahead.

**1:47** That warning goes to the people who move crews, pumps and planes: governments, fire agencies and community leaders. So resources get there before a fire starts, or while it's still small.

**2:02** The goal is not a faster evacuation. It's fewer of them.

**[PAUSE 1 second.]**

**2:08** To be clear, it doesn't predict where a fire will start. It shows when conditions are lining up, and why. Let us show you.

**[Turn to Speaker 3, who is already at the laptop with a hand on the trackpad. Speaker 1 switches the screen to the browser.]**

---

## SPEAKER 3: the live demo (2:20–3:50)

**[BEFORE GOING ON STAGE: site open at the home screen (the map). Header toggle on **Simulated**. Region in the panel: **Thunder Bay**. Browser full screen (F11), zoom 100%.]**

### Step 1: the panel (2:20–2:50)

**2:20** This is Before the Smoke. It's November, so fire season is over. What you're seeing is a simulated summer scenario, and the site says so right here.

**[Point at the big number in the panel.]**

**2:30** Thunder Bay District. Sixty-seven. High today. And tomorrow, Critical.

**2:37** Why? Five warning signs are on. Ten degrees hotter this week. Humidity down to twenty-nine percent. Six days without rain. Wind coming. And satellites already see fire nearby.

> *If the panel shows "Couldn't load data":* click **Simulated** in the top right. Still failing: play the screen recording and keep talking.

### Step 2: the map (2:50–3:00)

**[Sweep a hand across the coloured circles on the map.]**

**2:50** Every circle is a region, coloured by risk. The redder it is, the sooner crews and equipment should be there.

### Step 3: the real NASA imagery (3:00–3:25)

**[CLICK **NASA satellite · 14 July 2026** at the bottom of the map. Wait about two seconds for the imagery.]**

**3:03** Now this part is real. NASA satellite imagery from the fourteenth of July, the week of the evacuations. The red is fire seen from space. That haze is the smoke.

**3:15** And these labels are the First Nations that had to leave. Whitesand. Collins. Gull Bay.

> *If the imagery doesn't draw within five seconds:* say, "NASA's imagery for that day shows smoke across this whole region. It's in our recording." Click **Today** and move on.

### Step 4: the situation brief (3:25–3:50)

**[CLICK **Today**, then the orange **Send situation brief** button at the bottom of the panel.]**

**3:28** And this is the point. One click turns all of it into a situation brief for fire agencies and community leaders: the risk, the warning signs, the communities, and what to move in before the fire starts.

**[Let the brief sit on screen for two seconds. Then switch to SLIDE 4 and step away from the laptop. The movement tells the room the demo is over.]**

> *If the button isn't there:* say, "And it turns all of this into a one-page brief for fire agencies and community leaders," and step away.

### If the laptop or projector fails completely (about 25 seconds instead of the demo)

Speaker 3 stays calm and says this instead, then hands straight to Speaker 2:

> Our laptop has other plans, so picture it. Thunder Bay District. Ten degrees hotter in a week. Dry air. Six days without soaking rain. Satellites already see heat nearby. Our site flags it High today and Critical tomorrow, and turns that into a one-page brief for fire agencies and community leaders. Days before, not after.

You will finish about a minute early. Use the time to slow down. Do not add new material.

## SPEAKER 2: the ask (3:50–4:22)

**[SLIDE 4 is up: "5% of the population. 42% of wildfire evacuation events." Speaker 2 steps forward.]**

**3:50** So here's what we're asking for.

**3:53** One. Fund monitoring and early resources where the risk lines up, especially in the North, where many communities don't even have an air quality sensor.

**4:04** Two. Build tools like this with Indigenous leadership, not for them. Communities should own their data.

**4:11** Three. Treat this data as a shared responsibility. Open, honest about what it can't do, and in the hands of the people who need it.

**[Step back. Look at Speaker 1.]**

---

## SPEAKER 1: the close (4:22–4:40)

**4:22** Remember the number from the start? Five percent of the population. Forty-two percent of the evacuations.

**[PAUSE 2 seconds.]**

**4:32** The fires will come. Whether people see them coming, and get to stay home, is up to us.

**[Hold eye contact. Do not say thank you. Wait for the applause. END about 4:40.]**
