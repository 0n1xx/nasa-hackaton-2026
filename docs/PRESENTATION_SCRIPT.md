# BorealWatch — five-minute presentation script

Three speakers · about 610 spoken words · 5:00 including pauses and demo

Stage directions are in [BRACKETS]. Do not read them aloud.

---

## Speaker 1 — the problem (0:00–1:15)

**[SLIDE 1: black screen, one line: "What percentage of Canada's population is Indigenous?"]**

**0:00** Do you know what percentage of Canada's population is Indigenous?

**[PAUSE. Count ten seconds silently. Look at the audience. Do not fill the silence.]**

**0:14** Five percent.

**[PAUSE two seconds. SLIDE 2: "5%" then "42%"]**

**0:18** And that five percent experiences approximately forty-two percent of wildfire evacuation events in Canada.

**[PAUSE three seconds.]**

**0:28** That number comes from Dr. Amy Cardinal Christianson, a Métis fire scientist, and forty years of evacuation records. Some First Nations have been evacuated five times or more.

**0:40** This July it happened here. Fires north of Thunder Bay forced out Whitesand, Gull Bay, Collins and other First Nations. Families drove south to a city whose hotels were already full.

**0:54** And leaving is not simple. These are territories people have cared for over thousands of years. Home, culture, history and ceremony are in that land. So "just evacuate" cannot be the whole plan.

**1:07** We cannot stop every wildfire. But we can see risk building sooner. [TURN TO SPEAKER 2] So what would that take?

---

## Speaker 2 — the solution (1:15–2:35)

**[SLIDE 3: BorealWatch logo and tagline: "Track wildfires. Understand smoke. Protect communities."]**

**1:15** It takes putting the right information in one place. We built BorealWatch.

**1:22** It starts with NASA. Satellites carrying the VIIRS and MODIS instruments detect heat from fires across Canada every day, and NASA publishes daily imagery of the smoke.

**[SLIDE 4: seven icons — satellite, temperature, humidity, wind, rain, dry vegetation, air quality]**

**1:36** We combine those fire detections with Canadian fire agency reports and with the conditions that let fire spread: temperature, humidity, wind, recent rain, and how dry the vegetation and soil are. Then we add air quality, because smoke reaches far more people than flames do. And we bring in years of historical wildfire records, so today can be compared with the past.

**1:54** From this we calculate a risk index, from low to critical, for each region. It is deliberately simple. You can see every input and how many points each one adds.

**2:06** We want to be honest about what it is. It cannot tell you a fire will start at a particular place. It is a prototype that has not yet been validated against fire records. What it can do is show when conditions are lining up, days ahead, so crews and equipment can move before an emergency instead of after.

**2:28** [TURN TO SPEAKER 3] Let us show you.

---

## Speaker 3 — impact, demo and close (2:35–5:00)

**[OPEN THE WEBSITE — already loaded on the Overview page, "Simulated scenario" selected]**

**2:35** This is BorealWatch. It is November, so Canada's fire season is over. What you see is a simulated summer scenario, and the yellow banner says so on every page.

**[SHOW THE CANADA WILDFIRE MAP — scroll to "Canada at a glance"]**

**2:47** Three questions up top: where are the fires, which communities may be affected, and where is risk rising. Below, active fires, satellite detections, and community locations in green.

**[CLICK "Wildfire map" in the menu. SELECT THUNDER BAY in the Region box if not already chosen. CLICK the "14 Jul 2026" button.]**

**3:00** Here is Thunder Bay. And this layer is real: NASA imagery from the fourteenth of July, the week of the evacuations.

**[SHOW THE AIR QUALITY DASHBOARD — click "Air quality"]**

**3:12** Air quality. Fine smoke particles hour by hour, a three-day forecast, and plain guidance on who is affected first.

**[OPEN THE WILDFIRE RISK ASSESSMENT — click "Risk assessment"]**

**3:24** The risk assessment. The level, how confident we are, and exactly what is driving it. Here, dry air and no rain. And the seventy-two-hour outlook.

**[SHOW THE INDIGENOUS COMMUNITY IMPACT SECTION — click "Community impact", scroll to the table]**

**3:38** And the reason we built this. For each community: the nearest fire, the smoke, and which have faced this before. We show only public locations, and we do not speak for these Nations. Their data should be theirs.

**[STEP AWAY FROM THE LAPTOP. SLIDE 5: photo-free closing slide with the final line.]**

**3:55** BorealWatch is not really about tracking fires. It is about time. A few more days for a community to decide, on its own terms, how to protect its people, its homes, its ancestral lands and its cultural heritage.

**4:10** So we are asking for three things. Invest in monitoring before the fire, especially in the North, where many communities have no air sensor at all. Build tools like this with Indigenous leadership, not for them. And treat environmental data as a responsibility, labelled honestly and shared fairly.

**4:34** Five percent of the population. Forty-two percent of the evacuations.

**[PAUSE two seconds.]**

**4:42** The fires will come. Whether communities face them warned or unwarned is up to us.

**[Hold eye contact. Do not say thank you. END 4:50 — ten seconds of margin.]**
