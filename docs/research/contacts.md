## 4. How wildfire response on reserve works, in plain words

Sources: ISC service agreements page https://www.sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1669207681911/1669207720136 and each ISC regional plan (URLs in table). All fetched 2026-11-14.

| Province | Who fights fire on reserve | Who declares / who to call first | Source URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ontario | Ontario (AFFES, Ministry of Natural Resources) under a 1991 ISC wildfire management agreement that is ongoing. | First Nation (Chief and Council) initiates the request and declaration; a Joint Command Call with the community and ISC starts emergency declarations ("no wrong door"). For evacuations the Provincial Emergency Operations Centre (PEOC) coordinates once the First Nation requests one. Evacuation costs are reimbursable if Chief and Council and ISC agree and an event is present or imminent. No ISC duty number published. | https://www.sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1771600472528/1771600491104 |
| Manitoba | Manitoba Wildfire Service under an ISC agreement renewed annually. | First Nations leadership starts support by calling the ISC Duty Officer (number not published). Leadership can declare a state of local emergency or issue evacuation orders; ISC records these but they do not by themselves activate supports. | https://www.sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1771525921726 |
| Saskatchewan | Saskatchewan Public Safety Agency under the 1993 Canada-Saskatchewan Forest Fire Suppression Agreement (in effect indefinitely, ISC says new approach under discussion). | Chief and Council activate the community all-hazard plan; when overwhelmed, the First Nation contacts its emergency service provider, which can include ISC Regional Emergency Management (EMAP guidance). Leadership may declare a State of Local Emergency or order evacuations. SPSA runs an Emergency Operations Centre in fire season that First Nations can request resources from. | https://www.sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1771604966077 |

| Quebec | SOPFEU under a 2019 ISC agreement (surveillance and suppression on reserve, plus risk analysis and training). | Community emergency committee leads; ISC Quebec Region and W8banaki (liaison, Emergency Operations Centre in Wôlinak) coordinate; if exceeded, Quebec Ministry of Public Security helps. No phone given. | https://www.sac-isc.gc.ca/eng/1771598666984 |
| Northwest Territories | No ISC wildfire agreement listed for the NWT; the agreements page says costs can be recovered through EMAP response and recovery reimbursement. Plain version for a band office: Chief and Council (or the community emergency lead) activate the community plan and can declare a local emergency and order evacuation. Report the fire to the provincial or territorial number in section 1. Then contact the ISC regional emergency management office (section 3) and, where named above, the provincial emergency coordination centre (PEOC in Ontario, AEMA in Alberta, EMCR in BC, SPSA operations centre in Saskatchewan, ISC Duty Officer in Manitoba). Keep invoices, estimates and the "Declaration by Band" form for reimbursement.

## Plain-English summary: who to contact for what

- Fire burning right now, or smoke you cannot explain: call the provincial/territorial report line (section 1). If lives or homes are in immediate danger, call 911.
- Need to declare a local emergency or order an evacuation: Chief and Council do this. Then tell ISC regional emergency management and the provincial emergency centre (ON PEOC, AB AEMA, BC EMCR, SK SPSA, MB ISC Duty Officer).
- Need money for evacuation, response or recovery: ISC EMAP, through the regional office. Keep invoices and the Declaration by Band form.
- Need money or help for prevention (FireSmart, training, emergency plans): EMAP NSMP and FireSmart calls via ISC; in BC go through FNESS; in Saskatchewan SFNEM/PAGC offers training; NIFSC offers training and tools nationally.
- Checking today's fire danger or fire map: use the agency page in section 2.
- Smoke and health: AQHI (weather.gc.ca/airquality) and the Health Canada smoke page.
